Site Plan

WDD 231 final project – Eduar J Fajardo

Site Name

Campinas Trails

I chose this name because it says the two things a visitor needs to know before clicking: where the trails are, and what the site is about. Most people looking for a hike search for the name of their city first, so putting Campinas in the name helps the site be found.

Possible domain: campinas-trails.com

Site Purpose

Campinas Trails is a guide to hiking trails within about 80 kilometres of Campinas, in the state of São Paulo, Brazil. People who live here often do not know how many trails are close by, and the information that exists is spread across social media posts and old blogs.

The site will provide:

  • A list of trails, each one showing its distance, difficulty, walking time, the city it is in, and whether dogs are allowed.
  • A filter so a visitor can narrow the list down to the kind of hike they are looking for.
  • A way to save the trails they are interested in, so the list is still there when they come back.
  • A form where visitors can suggest a trail that is missing from the site.

Scenarios

These are questions a visitor would arrive with. The content of the site has to answer them.

  • Which trails near Campinas can I finish in less than two hours?
  • Is there a trail where I can bring my dog?
  • I have never hiked before. Which trail should I start with?

Color Scheme

I picked colours that suit an outdoor site: a dark green for structure, a warm clay tone for links and accents, and a soft cream background so the pages do not feel harsh to read.

The four colours and where each one is used
Colour Hex Where it is used
Forest green #2F5D3A Header and footer background, headings, table headers
Clay #A34E28 Links, the site name, and section borders
Cream #FAF7F0 Page background
Ink #262421 Body text and list items

I checked each pair with the WebAIM contrast checker so the text passes the AA level.

Typography

I am using two fonts, both from Google Fonts.

Pico do Atibaia – 6 km, moderate

Merriweather, bold. Used for all headings (h1, h2 and h3) and for the site name. It is a serif face, which gives the titles a calmer feel than a plain sans serif.

Every paragraph, label and navigation link.

Open Sans, regular and semibold. Used for body text, navigation, table contents and the form labels. It stays readable at small sizes on a phone, which matters because people will open this site outdoors.

Wireframe

These are my sketches of the home page. On a small screen everything sits in one column and the navigation collapses into a hamburger menu. On a wider screen the navigation opens into a row and the trail cards line up in a grid.

  • Small screen: header with the logo and a hamburger menu, a hero image below it, a filter control, then the trail cards stacked in a single column, and the footer at the bottom.
  • Large screen: the same header but with the navigation links in a row, the hero image across the full width, the filter in a horizontal bar, and the trail cards laid out in a three column grid above the footer.
